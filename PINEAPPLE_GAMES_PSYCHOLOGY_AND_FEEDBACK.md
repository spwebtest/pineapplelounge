# 🍍 Pineapple Games — Psychology & Member Feedback

**Purpose:** A practical reference for choosing, improving and reviewing games in the Pineapple Telegram community. Based on feedback discussed on 11 October 2026. This is an informal engagement analysis, **not** a clinical or scientific psychology report.

## 1. What members seem to enjoy

- **Low-friction participation:** Telegram polls, one-tap choices and short written answers are easy to join.
- **Guessing and curiosity:** Hidden images, clues and warmer/colder feedback invite repeat guesses.
- **Suspense and reveals:** Box openings, gradual reveals, race eliminations and delayed poll results give members a reason to return.
- **Social comparison:** Predicting what *the group* will choose is engaging, especially when there is no objectively correct answer.
- **Variety:** Repeating the same format too often can make it feel stale.
- **Optional creativity:** Photo edits, jokes and GIFs can work, but requiring everyone to write a sales pitch raises the effort needed.

**Practical design target:** Aim for a typical response that takes around **5–20 seconds**, while allowing members who want to engage more to do so. This is a design suggestion, not a measured statistic.

## 3. Working hypotheses about participation

| Engagement mechanism | Why it may help | Pineapple example |
|---|---|---|
| Low effort | More members can join quickly | A/B poll, one-word answer |
| Curiosity gap | People want to discover the hidden answer | Mystery Monday, Zoom In! |
| Progressive feedback | Each clue encourages another attempt | Hot or Cold, Square Reveal |
| Anticipation | Members return to see the outcome | Deal or No Deal, horse-race final |
| Group prediction | Creates friendly comparison without requiring expertise | Showdown Saturday, Pineapple Fortunes |
| Recognition and humour | Makes the community feel involved | GIF games, member-led specials |
| Novelty without overload | A familiar format with a fresh theme is easier to join | Teaser Tuesday picture categories |

These are **hypotheses and design principles**, not proven causal findings about the group's members.

## 4. Design rules for future games

1. Make the instructions understandable in one short Telegram message.
2. Prefer **tap, guess or answer briefly** as the default action.
3. Make participation possible even if a member joins late.
4. Use pictures, clues, polls and reveals to create interest.
5. Offer optional extra chat without making it compulsory.
6. Rotate formats so favourites remain fresh.
7. Reserve longer hosted games for **special events**.
8. Don't confuse the most messages with the most unique players.
9. When possible, track the amount of admin work needed.
10. Keep group humour welcoming and voluntary, especially in roasts or member-themed mysteries.

## 6. Weekly favourites vs special events

**Regular favourites:** Mystery Monday, Teaser Tuesday (trial), Where’s Scotty? Hot or Cold, Transform Thursday, Showdown Saturday.

**Rotating alternatives:** Telly Tuesday, Tunes Tuesday, Truth or Dare Thursday, GIF Friday, Pineapple Fortunes, Zoom In!, Porky Pineapple, Sunday Roast.

**Special events:** Deal or No Deal, Horse Racing, Murder Mystery, Pineapple Chase, Showdown Championship and larger wheel events.

No fixed dates are required for specials.

## 7. Simple evaluation after each game

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Date and game | When and what ran |
| Unique participants | How many different members joined |
| Total votes / guesses | Overall interaction |
| Return participation | Whether members came back for later rounds |
| Completion | How many finished, where relevant |
| Admin effort | Low / medium / high, or minutes spent |
| Member reaction | Brief notes on what people liked or disliked |
| Next decision | Repeat / rotate / revise / retire |

**Note:** Telegram screenshots may not always show every voter because of scrolling; record only verified counts and label incomplete data.

## 8. How to use this document

Before introducing a game, ask: **Can members understand it quickly? Can they join in seconds? Is there a reason to check back? Is it different enough from the last few games?**

Update the feedback table as new games are tested. Keep **observed member feedback** separate from **ideas and psychological hypotheses**.
